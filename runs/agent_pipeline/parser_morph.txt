Bone marrow aspirate and core biopsy (left posterior iliac crest) from a 58-year-old man with 41% circulating peripheral blood blasts show a markedly hypercellular marrow (approximately 90-95%) with extensive replacement by sheets of immature mononuclear cells and extensive blast-equivalent infiltration on the core; the 500-cell aspirate differential gives 18% blasts, with promonocytes (4%) enumerated separately. Blasts are medium-to-large with fine chromatin and one to three nucleoli, and a subset shows slightly folded or indented nuclei with grayish cytoplasm and occasional fine azurophilic granulation; no definite Auer rods are identified after review of multiple smears. Background erythropoiesis is left-shifted with mild megaloblastoid change, megakaryocytes are reduced, reticulin shows no significant fibrosis (MF-1), and storage iron is present without ring sideroblasts. Cytochemically, MPO is positive in only a minor subset of blasts (approximately 5-10%) and NSE is positive in a subset of cells with partial fluoride inhibition, a combined pattern the report reads as consistent with a myeloid process with probable monocytic differentiation, to be confirmed by flow cytometry. The report states that the 18% aspirate blast count is below the historical 20% threshold, that enumeration is likely affected by hemodilution and by the difficulty of separating promonocytes from myeloid blasts on smears, and that promonocyte assignment is morphologically subjective. It further states that monocytic lineage is probable only and that classification depends on flow cytometry, cytogenetic, and molecular results that were pending at the time of the report.
