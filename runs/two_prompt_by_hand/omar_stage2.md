## 3. Clinical context

A 58-year-old man presented with three weeks of progressive fatigue, exertional dyspnea, gingival bleeding and scattered lower-extremity ecchymoses. CBC: WBC 38.4 x10^9/L, hemoglobin 7.9 g/dL, platelets 31 x10^9/L. The peripheral smear showed 41% circulating blasts, some with folded nuclei and abundant cytoplasm. He has no prior hematologic history and no prior chemotherapy or radiation. The marrow was performed to evaluate for acute leukemia.

## 4. Morphology summary

- **Aspirate:** Blasts 18% by 500-cell manual differential. Promonocytes (4%) were counted separately from blasts. No definite Auer rods after review of multiple smears.
- **Core biopsy:** Markedly hypercellular (approximately 90-95%). Immature mononuclear cells replace the marrow in sheets, with interstitial and paratrabecular distribution and extensive blast-equivalent infiltration.
- **Cytochemistry:** MPO positive in a minor subset of blasts (approximately 5-10%). NSE (alpha-naphthyl butyrate) positive in a subset of cells with partial fluoride inhibition, which supports a monocytic component. Reticulin MF-1, without significant fibrosis. Storage iron present; no ring sideroblasts.
- **Background hematopoiesis:** Erythropoiesis is left-shifted with mild megaloblastoid change. Megakaryocytes are reduced, and markedly reduced on the core.
- **Lineage:** Myeloid, with probable monocytic differentiation by morphology and cytochemistry. The report defers confirmation to flow cytometry and notes that promonocytes are hard to separate from immature myeloid forms on smears.
- **Morphologic diagnosis:** Hypercellular marrow with acute myeloid leukemia (with comment).
- **Comment carried forward:** The report states that under current WHO and ICC classifications a 20% blast count is not required for AML when a defining genetic abnormality is present.

## 5. Flow cytometry summary

- **Blasts:** Abnormal myeloid blast population, approximately 22% of total nucleated cells by CD45/side scatter gating.
- **Blast phenotype:** CD34 positive (subset, about 60%), CD117+, HLA-DR bright, CD13+, CD33 bright, CD38+, cytoplasmic MPO dim subset, CD64+, CD4 dim, CD11b partial/variable, CD14 negative. Aberrant CD56 (partial) and CD7 (subset). CD2, CD19 and cytoplasmic CD3 negative.
- **Monocytic compartment:** Markedly expanded and left-shifted, approximately 30% of TNC (maturing monocytes 19%, promonocytes/immature monocytic cells 11%). These cells express bright uniform CD64, CD14 on the maturing fraction, CD11b, CD11c, CD36, HLA-DR, dim CD4, CD13 and CD33. They are CD34 negative, with partial CD56.
- **Lymphocytes:** Polytypic, no aberrancy.
- **Conclusion:** Diagnostic of acute myeloid leukemia with monocytic differentiation. The immunophenotype establishes the monocytic lineage that morphology only favored.

## 6. Cytogenetics summary

- **AML FISH panel (all negative):** RUNX1-RUNX1T1 t(8;21) 0.5%; CBFB inv(16)/t(16;16) 1.0%; PML-RARA t(15;17) 0.5%; KMT2A 11q23.3 rearrangement 1.5%; MECOM 3q26.2 rearrangement 1.0%; EGR1 -5/5q- 1.5%; D7S486 -7/7q- 1.0%; TP53 17p deletion 2.0%.

## 8. Integrated interpretation

1. Morphology and flow cytometry independently identify acute myeloid leukemia.
2. Morphology considered monocytic differentiation probable on the basis of monocytoid cells and NSE positivity with partial fluoride inhibition, and it left the lineage call to flow cytometry.
3. Flow cytometry confirms monocytic differentiation: an expanded, left-shifted monocytic compartment of about 30% of nucleated cells expresses bright CD64, CD11b, CD11c, CD36 and dim CD4, with CD14 on the maturing fraction.
4. This difference in lineage certainty is not a true conflict, because immunophenotyping is the more specific method and the morphology report asked for flow confirmation.
5. Both methods agree that MPO marks only a minor subset of blasts.
6. The marrow blast percentage does conflict: 18% by 500-cell manual aspirate differential and 22% by flow gating.
7. The flow report states that manual and gated counts are expected to differ, and the morphology report attributes its lower figure to hemodilution and to the difficulty of separating promonocytes from myeloid blasts on smears.
8. The core biopsy, 90-95% cellular with sheets of immature mononuclear cells, shows infiltration well above either number.
9. Both values are reported as measured, and neither is forced into a single figure.
10. The two counts straddle the historical 20% threshold, and the manual count falls below it.
11. That tension does not decide the case.
12. NGS detected an NPM1 type A frameshift insertion at a VAF of 39%.
13. Both the morphology and molecular reports state that under WHO and ICC a 20% blast count is not required when a defining genetic abnormality such as mutated NPM1 is present.
14. The NPM1 mutation was detectable only by NGS and was invisible to morphology, flow cytometry and cytogenetics, so the defining abnormality in this case came from a single modality.
15. Cytogenetics supplied the necessary exclusions: the karyotype is 46,XY[20], and FISH is negative for PML-RARA, RUNX1-RUNX1T1, CBFB, KMT2A and MECOM rearrangements and for 5q, 7q and 17p loss.
16. Only cytogenetics could exclude these lesions, since the NGS assay does not reliably detect large structural rearrangements or copy-number changes.
17. No competing cytogenetically defined AML entity or adverse cytogenetic lesion is present, which fits the cytogenetics report's statement that cytogenetically normal AML is often driven by gene-level mutations.
18. FLT3-ITD (VAF 31%, allelic ratio 0.62) was also seen only by NGS.
19. It does not change the disease category, but it is prognostically adverse and is a target for FLT3 inhibitor therapy.
20. DNMT3A R882H (VAF 44%) was detected only by NGS as well.
21. The molecular report states that it does not by itself define or change the disease category, and it plays no part in this classification.
22. Partial CD56 and subset CD7 expression on blasts was seen only by flow cytometry.
23. These aberrancies do not alter the diagnosis, but they give a leukemia-associated immunophenotype that may be useful for measurable residual disease tracking.

## 9. Final integrated diagnosis

1. Bone marrow, left posterior iliac crest: acute myeloid leukemia with mutated NPM1 (WHO and ICC), with monocytic differentiation established by flow cytometry.
2. FLT3 internal tandem duplication is present (VAF 31%, ITD/WT allelic ratio 0.62) as a prognostically adverse co-mutation.
3. Marrow blasts are 18% by manual aspirate differential and 22% by flow cytometry; a 20% blast count is not required for this entity.
4. The karyotype is normal male, 46,XY[20], and the AML FISH panel is negative.

## 10. Prognostic and predictive notes

- **FLT3-ITD:** The molecular report states it is prognostically adverse and a target for FLT3 inhibitor therapy. The ITD/WT allelic ratio of 0.62 is reported to support treatment decisions.
- **DNMT3A R882H (VAF 44%):** Non-classifying. It is commonly co-mutated with NPM1 and FLT3-ITD and is often a clonal hematopoiesis founder event. The molecular report lists it for possible prognostic relevance only.
- **Negative findings with risk relevance:** No TP53 variant by NGS and no 17p deletion by FISH. No -5/5q-, -7/7q-, KMT2A or MECOM abnormality by FISH. No RUNX1, ASXL1 or KIT variant by NGS.
- **Risk tier:** None of the component reports assigns an explicit cytogenetic or integrated risk tier. This report does not assign one.
- **Residual disease monitoring:** The flow report notes that aberrant CD56/CD7 expression may be useful for measurable residual disease tracking.

## 11. Limitations and pending or recommended studies

**Limitations stated by the component reports**
- Morphology: the manual aspirate blast count (18%) is likely lowered by hemodilution and by difficulty separating promonocytes from myeloid blasts. The morphology component was not intended to stand alone.
- Flow cytometry: the blast estimate (22%) differs from the manual count, as expected for gated versus manual methods. Laboratory-developed test.
- Cytogenetics: a normal karyotype does not exclude a submicroscopic abnormality.
- Molecular: analytical sensitivity is about 5% VAF for SNVs and small indels. The assay does not reliably detect large structural rearrangements, copy-number changes, or variants in low-coverage or high-homology regions. A negative result does not exclude a mutation below the limit of detection. Laboratory-developed test, not FDA cleared or approved.

**Uncertainties flagged during data extraction**
- Monocyte counts differ between modalities and were not reconciled. Morphology reports 6% mature monocytes and 4% promonocytes, while flow reports 19% maturing monocytes and 11% promonocytes. The integrated diagnosis does not depend on either set, but the discrepancy is recorded.
- Report dates are recorded as printed. The morphology report (2026-04-24) states ancillary results were pending even though flow was issued 2026-04-23.
- The FLT3-ITD has no HGVS designation in the source report; a descriptive designation is used.
- Some NPM1 and FLT3 table rows were split during PDF text extraction and reassembled by column position. The HGVS, consequence and VAF values should be checked against the original molecular report before sign-out.
- The case ID was not stated in any source report and was taken from the schema example. Riverbend laboratory names are paraphrased from report headers. The morphology footer reads "Page 1 of 1" although two pages of content were extracted and used.
- The flow monocytic percentage (about 30%) is the sum of the reported 19% and 11% fractions.

**Pending or recommended studies**
- All four component studies are final. No studies are pending.
- The aberrant CD56/CD7 immunophenotype is available as a baseline for measurable residual disease tracking by flow cytometry.
