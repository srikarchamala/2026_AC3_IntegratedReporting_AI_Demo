## Integrated Interpretation

The findings agree across all four modalities and support a diagnosis of **acute myeloid leukemia with mutated *NPM1***.

- **Establishing AML.** The core biopsy shows near-total marrow replacement by immature cells, there are 41% circulating blasts, and flow cytometry shows a discrete abnormal myeloid blast population with aberrant antigen expression. Together these establish a myeloid neoplasm with a high blast burden. The aspirate count of 18% is very likely an underestimate (see below).
- **Lineage and differentiation.** Morphology (folded nuclei, grayish cytoplasm), cytochemistry (NSE+, weak MPO), and flow cytometry (expanded CD64-bright, CD11b/CD11c/CD36+, CD4-dim monocytic compartment with an immature fraction) all show **myelomonocytic or monocytic differentiation**. That pattern is typical of *NPM1*-mutated AML. WHO 2022 and ICC 2022 no longer subclassify NPM1-mutated AML by differentiation. The monocytic features are still worth describing, because they help explain the blast-count variability, they may matter for MRD gating, and they have possible therapeutic relevance (for example, monocytic AML has been associated with reduced venetoclax sensitivity in some studies).
- **Genetic classification.** A type A *NPM1* frameshift at VAF 39% is consistent with a heterozygous mutation in most of the marrow cells. It is a disease-defining abnormality. The normal karyotype and negative FISH panel are the expected cytogenetic context for NPM1-mutated AML, and they rule out competing or higher-priority defining lesions (PML::RARA, core-binding factor fusions, KMT2A, MECOM rearrangements). They also rule out adverse cytogenetics that would change risk (−5/5q−, −7/7q−, 17p).
- **No myelodysplasia-related or therapy-related features.** There is no history of cytotoxic therapy, no MDS-defining cytogenetics, and no MDS-related gene mutations. In any case, *NPM1* mutation takes precedence in both classification systems.
- **Clonal architecture.** The VAF ranking is *DNMT3A* (44%) > *NPM1* (39%) > *FLT3*-ITD (31%). This fits the usual pattern of a founder *DNMT3A* R882 clone, which may represent prior clonal hematopoiesis, followed by *NPM1* and then a subclonal *FLT3*-ITD. The *DNMT3A* mutation does not change the WHO/ICC category, but it is relevant to MRD strategy. *DNMT3A* frequently persists in remission and should **not** be used as an MRD marker.

## Discrepancies, Caveats, and Points for Reviewer Attention

1. **The blast count differs across methods.** It needs reconciling, and the sign-out should state which count it relies on.

   | Source | Blasts | Blasts + promonocytes (blast equivalents) |
   |---|---|---|
   | Peripheral blood (clinical history) | 41% | Not separately reported |
   | Aspirate manual differential | 18% | **22%** (18 + 4) |
   | Flow cytometry (% TNC) | 22% | **33%** (22 + 11) |
   | Core biopsy | "Extensive," not quantified | Near-total replacement at 90–95% cellularity |

   - The morphology comment calls the 18% "below the historical 20% threshold" but does not add the separately counted promonocytes. Under WHO and ICC, **promonocytes are blast equivalents**. Including them gives 22% on the aspirate, so the case is at or above 20% by every measure.
   - Flow percentages are not a substitute for the morphologic blast count. A flow blast gate also may not capture CD34−/CD117− promonocytes, and here those were counted in the monocytic gate. Flow agrees with the other studies, but it should not be the number of record.
   - Possible hemodilution of the aspirate is stated as a likely explanation but is not documented objectively (for example, by comparing with the PB count or noting spicule cellularity).
   - **Practical effect:** classification does not depend on resolving this. WHO 2022 has no blast cutoff for *NPM1*-mutated AML, and the ICC threshold (≥10%) is met by every method. The final report should still give a single reconciled blast + promonocyte percentage.
   - The morphology report's diagnosis line ("acute myeloid leukemia") was issued **before** genetic results were available, when the aspirate count was 18%. Strictly, AML at that point rested on the core biopsy, PB blasts, and blast equivalents rather than on the 18% aspirate count. The integrated report supersedes this.

2. ***CD34 expression is atypical for NPM1-mutated AML.*** Most *NPM1*-mutated AMLs are CD34-negative. Here about 60% of the blast gate is CD34+, and CD7 is aberrantly expressed. This is a recognized minority phenotype, not an inconsistency, and the type A *NPM1* call is unambiguous. It is still worth noting: some literature associates CD34 positivity in *NPM1*-mutated AML with *FLT3*-ITD and less favorable outcome, and it affects MRD panel design.

3. **The risk language differs between the NGS report and current ELN guidance.** The NGS report describes *FLT3*-ITD as "prognostically adverse" and reports the allelic ratio "to support treatment decisions." Under **ELN 2022**, the *FLT3*-ITD allelic ratio is no longer used for risk stratification, and ***NPM1*-mutated AML with *FLT3*-ITD is Intermediate risk** whatever the AR, provided adverse cytogenetics are absent, as they are here. The *FLT3*-ITD is still clinically important as a target for FLT3 inhibitors. The integrated report should use the current ELN category, and any differing wording should be reconciled with the treating team.

4. **Minor cytochemistry note.** NSE positivity with *partial* fluoride inhibition is reported. Classic monocytic esterase activity is typically fluoride-*sensitive*, meaning largely inhibited. The partial inhibition probably reflects a mixed granulocytic and monocytic population. It does not change the interpretation, since flow cytometry independently establishes the monocytic lineage.

5. **Limitations of the ancillary studies.**
   - Karyotype: 20 metaphases at 400–425 bphs, with 5 fully karyotyped. A small subclone or a cryptic or submicroscopic lesion cannot be excluded. FISH was limited to the panel probes.
   - NGS: limit of detection about 5% VAF. It does not reliably detect structural variants or CNVs or assess low-coverage regions. Fusions such as *NUP98* or *KMT2A*-PTD are not evaluated, and none are suggested here. It is a non-FDA-cleared LDT. *FLT3*-TKD is not explicitly addressed in the listed results. *FLT3* is not on the "no reportable variant" list, so whether D835/I836 TKD mutations were assessed should be confirmed.
   - Germline status of the variants cannot be determined from a marrow-only assay. That is not a specific concern here, since these are classic somatic AML lesions, but it is a standard limitation.
   - Peripheral blood blast percentage and CBC values come from the clinical history. No separate PB smear report was provided.

6. **Administrative and process notes.**
   - The morphology report says the ancillary studies were submitted "under separate accession," but the NGS report lists the morphology accession (S26-BM-01187) as its client accession. This is probably just the referring-lab cross-reference, but the linkage should be confirmed.
   - Identifiers (name, MRN, DOB, collection date) agree across all four reports.
   - Timeline: flow 04-23, morphology 04-24, cytogenetics 04-29, NGS 05-04. The NGS specimen was received at the reference lab on 04-25, three days after collection. The final integrated diagnosis could not be issued before 05-04.

## Suggested Comments and Recommendations (for reviewer consideration)

- **MRD:** *NPM1* type A quantitative RT-qPCR is the preferred MRD marker (peripheral blood after cycle 2 and marrow at end of treatment, per ELN MRD recommendations). The aberrant CD56/CD7 (and CD34+) blast phenotype supports flow-based MRD as a complementary method. *DNMT3A* R882H should not be used for MRD.
- ***FLT3*-ITD:** clinically actionable (FLT3 inhibitor eligibility). Treatment selection rests with the treating team.
- Consider confirming whether *FLT3*-TKD was assessed.
- Recommend clinical correlation. This integrated report supersedes the preliminary diagnostic statements in the individual component reports.
