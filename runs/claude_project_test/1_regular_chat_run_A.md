## Integrated interpretation

All four studies point to the same diagnosis. Morphology and the core biopsy show a markedly hypercellular marrow replaced by immature myelomonocytic cells. Flow cytometry confirms an aberrant myeloid blast population and an expanded, immature monocytic compartment. Karyotype and FISH exclude the other recurrent AML-defining rearrangements and the main adverse copy-number changes. NGS finds a type A **NPM1** mutation, which is disease-defining in both WHO 2022 and ICC 2022.

The myelomonocytic picture fits NPM1-mutated AML well. So do the folded or "cup-like" nuclei in the peripheral blood and marrow, the weak or partial MPO staining, and the monocytic differentiation on flow. The FLT3-ITD and DNMT3A R882 mutations make up the classic NPM1/FLT3-ITD/DNMT3A "triple-mutant" pattern. In that pattern the DNMT3A variant usually arises first (founder clone), with NPM1 and then FLT3-ITD acquired later.

**Clinically relevant points for the treating team:**
- FLT3-ITD is an actionable target for FLT3 inhibitor therapy (for example, with intensive induction).
- The NPM1 type A mutation is a good target for molecular MRD monitoring by quantitative PCR. A pretreatment baseline is advisable.
- Aberrant CD56 and CD7 on the blasts give a usable leukemia-associated immunophenotype for flow MRD.
- No adverse-risk cytogenetic or myelodysplasia-related gene mutation was found. TP53 is wild-type by NGS and FISH.

## Discrepancies and points needing reconciliation

1. **Blast percentage differs by method.**
   - Aspirate manual count: 18% blasts. If promonocytes are counted as blast equivalents (as WHO/ICC do in AML with monocytic differentiation), the total is about **22%**.
   - Flow: 22% myeloid blasts plus 11% promonocytes/immature monocytic cells, so about **33%** blast equivalents.
   - Peripheral blood: 41% blasts.
   - The core biopsy shows extensive replacement, well above 20%.
   - *Assessment:* The aspirate count is probably low because of hemodilution and because promonocytes are hard to tell apart from myeloid blasts on smears. The two reports describe "18%" and "22%" as values that "differ modestly," but they are not measuring the same thing. The draft final report should state one blast/blast-equivalent figure and explain how it was derived. **This does not change the diagnosis:** WHO 2022 has no 20% minimum for AML with NPM1 mutation, ICC requires ≥10%, and every measurement here is above both thresholds.

2. **Promonocyte count: 4% on morphology vs 11% on flow.** Flow gates by phenotype, and the morphology report itself flags promonocyte assignment as subjective. A reviewer should consider recounting promonocytes on the smears, or at least state how they were handled in the final blast-equivalent count.

3. **Wording about blast thresholds is incomplete.** The morphology and NGS reports both say that a 20% blast count is not required. That holds for WHO 2022, but **ICC 2022 still requires ≥10% blasts** for AML with mutated NPM1. The case meets both criteria, but the integrated report should state the threshold precisely.

4. **CD34 expression.** About 60% of the blasts are CD34+. NPM1-mutated AML is often CD34-negative, though not always. This is not a true discrepancy, and the molecular result is definitive.

5. **DNMT3A VAF (44%).** This is close to 50% and higher than the NPM1 VAF. That fits a founder or clonal hematopoiesis event, but a **germline** variant cannot be ruled out from tumor-only sequencing. Germline DNMT3A R882 is rare, so this is a low concern, but the integrated report could mention it. Germline confirmation is not needed for classification. DNMT3A is also the DNMT3A variant that tends to persist in remission, so it should not be used for MRD.

6. **Timing.** The morphology report (04-24) and the flow report (04-23) were signed before the cytogenetics (04-29) and NGS (05-04) results. The provisional wording in those reports ("to be confirmed," "classification pending") is replaced by this integrated report.

7. **Accession cross-reference.** The morphology report says the ancillary studies were sent "under separate accession." The NGS report gives the morphology accession (S26-BM-01187) as the client accession. This is consistent, but someone should confirm the specimen chain of custody between Riverbend and Meridian.

## Limitations

- **Karyotype:** only 20 metaphases were analyzed and 5 fully karyotyped, at 400–425 band resolution. Small or subclonal abnormalities cannot be excluded.
- **FISH** was done on *cultured* cells and covers a targeted panel only. It cannot detect rearrangements outside the panel, such as NUP98 or other cryptic fusions.
- **NGS** has about 5% VAF sensitivity and does not reliably detect structural variants or copy-number changes. **FLT3-TKD (e.g., D835) status is not stated explicitly** in the report and should be confirmed with the laboratory. The FLT3-ITD allelic ratio comes from fragment analysis, and ITD size/ratio estimates can vary between methods.
- **Laboratory-developed tests:** the flow and NGS assays are LDTs and are not FDA-cleared.
- **Clinical data** such as LDH, coagulation studies (DIC screen, given the bleeding), extramedullary disease (gums, skin, CNS) and performance status were not supplied. These matter for management but not for classification.
- **Hemodilution** of the aspirate may affect every aspirate-based percentage, including flow.

## Suggested comments / next steps (for reviewer consideration)

- Settle on one blast/blast-equivalent percentage that includes promonocytes, and document the method.
- Confirm FLT3-TKD status.
- Confirm that a pretreatment NPM1 quantitative PCR baseline is available for MRD.
- Note CD56/CD7 aberrancy for the flow MRD template.
- Optionally recommend germline evaluation if the DNMT3A VAF or the clinical or family history raises concern.
