## Summary by Modality

### 1. Morphology (S26-BM-01187)
- **Core biopsy:** markedly hypercellular (about 90-95%), with sheets of immature mononuclear cells replacing the marrow. Residual hematopoiesis is markedly decreased, megakaryocytes are markedly reduced, and there is no significant fibrosis (MF-1).
- **Aspirate:** 18% blasts on a 500-cell differential, plus 4% promonocytes counted separately. Blasts have fine chromatin and prominent nucleoli, and a subset has folded nuclei and grayish cytoplasm. No Auer rods were seen. Erythropoiesis is left-shifted with mild megaloblastoid change. No ring sideroblasts.
- **Cytochemistry:** MPO positive in about 5-10% of blasts. NSE positive in a subset, with partial fluoride inhibition.
- **Impression:** AML with probable monocytic differentiation. The pathologist notes the aspirate count is probably underestimated because of hemodilution and the difficulty of separating promonocytes from blasts.

### 2. Flow Cytometry (FC-26-003942)
- **Myeloid blasts: 22% of total nucleated cells.** Phenotype: CD34+ (about 60%), CD117+, HLA-DR bright, CD13+, CD33 bright, CD38+, dim cytoplasmic MPO, CD64+, dim CD4, partial CD11b, CD14-negative. Aberrant **CD56 (partial)** and **CD7 (subset)**. Negative for CD2, CD19, and cyCD3.

- **Impression:** diagnostic of AML with monocytic differentiation. Flow confirms the monocytic lineage that morphology could only favor.

## Integrated Interpretation

All four studies agree on a single process:

1. **AML is established.** The marrow is replaced on core biopsy, there are 41% peripheral blasts, and flow shows an abnormal myeloid blast population with aberrant CD56/CD7.
2. **The leukemia is monocytic.** Morphology (folded nuclei, NSE positivity with fluoride inhibition) suggested monocytic differentiation, and flow confirmed it (an expanded immature monocytic compartment with bright CD64, CD11b, CD36, and dim CD4). This matches the phenotype typical of NPM1-mutated AML: weak or partial CD34 and MPO, a prominent monocytic component, and cup-like or folded nuclear forms.
3. **The genetics define the entity.** The karyotype is normal and FISH is negative. The NGS panel found a type A NPM1 mutation, which the reference laboratory states is a WHO and ICC disease-defining abnormality.
4. **Reconciling the blast counts.** The values differ: 18% on the aspirate manual count (blasts only), 22% by flow, 41% in peripheral blood, and much higher on the core. The pathologist and flow director both explain this as hemodilution and the subjective separation of promonocytes from blasts. **This difference does not affect classification.** Because NPM1-mutated AML is defined genetically, the 20% blast threshold is not required, as the morphology and molecular reports both state.
5. **The DNMT3A R882H variant** has the highest VAF (44%) and probably represents a clonal hematopoiesis founder event. The NPM1 and FLT3-ITD variants appear to be later events in the leukemic clone. The molecular report states that DNMT3A does not change the disease category.

## Final Diagnosis

**BONE MARROW, LEFT POSTERIOR ILIAC CREST (ASPIRATE, CORE BIOPSY, AND ANCILLARY STUDIES):**

**ACUTE MYELOID LEUKEMIA WITH MUTATED NPM1 (WHO / ICC), with monocytic differentiation.**
- Concurrent **FLT3 internal tandem duplication** (VAF 31%, allelic ratio 0.62).
- Concurrent **DNMT3A R882H** (VAF 44%).
- Normal male karyotype, 46,XY[20]; negative AML FISH panel.
- Hypercellular marrow (90-95%) with extensive leukemic infiltration. Blasts 18% (aspirate manual count) and 22% (flow), with 41% in peripheral blood.

## Prognostic and Management Notes

These notes summarize what the source reports state.

- **FLT3-ITD:** the molecular report calls it prognostically adverse and a target for FLT3 inhibitor therapy. The allelic ratio of 0.62 is reported to support treatment decisions.
- **NPM1 mutation:** this is the disease-defining lesion. A specific type A variant has been identified, so it could also serve as a molecular MRD marker. The reports do not explicitly say this, so confirm with the molecular laboratory.
- **DNMT3A R882H:** frequently co-occurs with NPM1 and FLT3-ITD. The report lists it for completeness and "potential prognostic relevance" but does not state a specific effect.
- **Adverse cytogenetic and molecular lesions not found:** TP53, KMT2A, MECOM, -5/5q-, -7/7q-, and the ASXL1, RUNX1, and splicing-factor mutations tested on the panel were all negative.
- **MRD tracking:** the flow report notes that aberrant CD56 and CD7 expression may help with flow-based MRD monitoring.
- **Not covered by the provided reports:** a formal risk category (for example, ELN classification), specific therapy recommendations, and survival estimates. These should be determined by the treating hematology/oncology team.

## Limitations
- The NGS assay reliably detects variants down to about 5% VAF. It does not reliably detect structural or copy-number changes, and a negative result does not exclude low-level mutations.
- A normal karyotype at 400-425 band resolution does not exclude submicroscopic abnormalities.
- Each component report states that it is not meant to stand alone. This integrated summary combines them, but it should be reviewed and signed by the responsible hematopathologist before clinical use.
