## Integrated Interpretation

The four studies agree with each other and point to one diagnosis:

1. **Leukemia is established.** The marrow is 90-95% cellular with sheets of immature cells, and there are 41% blasts in the peripheral blood. Flow cytometry found an abnormal myeloid blast population with an aberrant phenotype (CD56, CD7).
2. **The lineage is myeloid with monocytic differentiation.** Morphology and NSE cytochemistry suggested it. Flow cytometry confirmed it, showing an expanded, left-shifted monocytic compartment (about 30% of TNC) with bright CD64, CD11b, CD11c, CD36, and dim CD4.
3. **No recurrent cytogenetic abnormality was found.** The karyotype is normal and the FISH panel is negative, so no karyotype- or FISH-defined AML subtype applies.
4. **The defining genetic lesion is NPM1 mutation.** As the molecular report states, mutated NPM1 is a WHO- and ICC-recognized disease-defining abnormality. Because of this, **the 20% blast threshold is not required.** This settles the borderline aspirate blast count (18%). It is also worth noting that blast estimates in this case are all at or near threshold or above it: 18% manual aspirate, 22% by flow, and 41% in peripheral blood.

**Notes on the discrepancies between reports (none change the diagnosis):**
- **Blast percentage:** 18% (manual aspirate), 22% (flow), and 41% (peripheral blood). The flow report says gating and manual counts are expected to differ, and that either value supports the diagnosis. The morphology report attributes the low aspirate count to hemodilution and to the difficulty of classifying promonocytes.
- **Monocytic burden:** morphology counted about 10% monocytic cells (6% mature plus 4% promonocytes), while flow counted about 30%. This fits the morphologist's stated difficulty in identifying promonocytes on smears. Flow cytometry is the more objective measure of lineage here.
- **Allele frequencies:** the DNMT3A VAF (44%) is higher than the NPM1 (39%) and FLT3-ITD (31%) VAFs. This fits the molecular report's comment that DNMT3A R882H is often a founder (clonal hematopoiesis) event, with NPM1 and FLT3-ITD arising later in the clone.

## Prognostic and Therapeutic Notes

These notes use only what the source reports state:

- **NPM1 mutation:** this is the disease-defining lesion, and it occurs here with a normal karyotype.
- **FLT3-ITD:** the molecular report calls it **prognostically adverse** and a **target for FLT3 inhibitor therapy**. The allelic ratio (0.62) is reported to support treatment decisions.
- **DNMT3A R882H:** commonly co-mutated with NPM1 and FLT3-ITD and often a clonal hematopoiesis founder event. It does not change the WHO/ICC category and is reported for completeness and *potential* prognostic relevance.
- **Adverse cytogenetic lesions were not found:** FISH was negative for KMT2A, MECOM, -5/5q-, -7/7q-, and TP53 deletion, and NGS found no TP53, RUNX1, or ASXL1 variants.
- **Measurable residual disease (MRD):** the flow report says the aberrant CD56/CD7 expression may be useful for MRD tracking.
- **Formal risk category:** none of the four reports assigns a formal risk group (for example, an ELN category). The treating team should determine it using current guidelines.

## Limitations
- The aspirate blast count may be underestimated because of hemodilution.
- A normal karyotype does not exclude submicroscopic abnormalities.
- The NGS assay has about 5% VAF sensitivity and does not reliably detect large structural rearrangements or copy-number changes. A negative result does not exclude mutations below the detection limit.
- The flow and NGS assays are laboratory-developed tests; the NGS assay is not FDA-cleared.
